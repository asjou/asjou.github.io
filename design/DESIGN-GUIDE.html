01Intent

A records index that reads like a late edition

What the site is. A public index of FCC equipment-authorization records: public notices, orders to show cause, and Covered List actions. Every entry is a real document with a date and a docket number. The page lists them newest first and sends the reader to the source.

What the design does. It borrows the discipline of a printed newspaper of record and none of its decoration. Dark ground, cool ink, one accent, three typefaces with one job each, and a ledger column that makes dates and dockets scannable down one edge.

Four principles. Every choice in this guide follows from them.

  • Restraint over ornament. No dinkus, no printer's mark, no corner brackets, no glow. The ruling system is the only decoration.
  • Records are equals. Entries sit in date order at one size. There is no lead story, because a docket is not news.
  • One accent, one meaning. Jade marks structure and action. Vermilion marks enforcement risk and nothing else.
  • Type does the work. Hierarchy comes from face, size, and colour, never from boxes, chips, or badges.
02Colour

Cool ink on neutral black, jade in the rules

The ground is a neutral near-black, not a warm brown or a blue navy. Ink is a cool white. Jade appears only as hairlines, the active navigation marker, link hover, and the one button. Vermilion appears only on the Bad Labs tag. Gold is retired.

03Type

Three faces, one job each

Poiret One sets the wordmark and nothing else. EB Garamond sets titles, summaries, the tagline, and every label as hand-set small caps. Courier Prime sets dates and docket numbers and nothing else. All three load from Google Fonts with real fallback stacks.

Small caps recipe

The fonts ship no true small caps, so set them by hand and they render the same everywhere: uppercase, size at 0.78 of the parent, letter-spacing 0.10em. Use this for kickers, navigation, the folio, the newsletter heading, and the colophon.

Measure and wrapping

  • Titles run to 640px and use text-wrap: balance, so a two-line title breaks evenly.
  • Summaries run to 600px and are clamped to two lines. Write them to fit. Do not rely on the clamp to cut.
  • Product names with hyphens use a non-breaking hyphen, so "CopyPaste‑2" never splits at the line end.
  • Dates are ISO in the ledger and spelled out in prose: "2026-07-28" beside the entry, "28 July 2026" in the strip.
04Layout

One centred column with a ledger margin

The whole page lives in a centred column. Inside it, a fixed margin column carries the date and docket, a vertical hairline runs the full height of the list, and the body column carries everything else. Entries are separated by space alone. There are no rules between them.

06Rules

Do this, not that

Eleven iterations and twelve independent critiques settled these. Each rule on the right cost a point at some stage.

Do

  • Keep every entry the same size and in date order.
  • Use jade for rules, the active item, hover, and the button.
  • Reserve vermilion for enforcement-risk tags.
  • Set labels as hand-made small caps in the serif.
  • Keep dates and dockets in mono, in the margin.
  • Separate entries with space and the vertical hairline.
  • Write summaries to two lines.
  • Keep grain at 8% and the vignette soft.

Do not

  • Add a lead story, hero, or featured card.
  • Add ornaments: dinkus, monogram, corner brackets, glow.
  • Use gold, amber, or any warm accent.
  • Put labels in chips, badges, or boxes.
  • Mix a third face into labels or navigation.
  • Draw rules between entries.
  • Show a constant tag such as "FCC" on every row.
  • Duplicate Subscribe in the navigation.

Texture

One grain layer at 8% opacity in screen blend, from an SVG turbulence filter, sits over the whole page and ignores the pointer. A radial vignette darkens the corners of the ground. Both are felt, not seen.
